THE BUILDING THESIS
01 / PHYSICS

Does it predict real contact?

Replay measured motion. Compare force, contact geometry, and sensor output on reserved objects and trajectories.

Hypothesis to test
02 / COMPUTE

Is it practical to run?

Report full-step latency and throughput with GPU, resolution, batch size, and memory. Compare matched baselines.

Benchmark needed
03 / UTILITY

Does synthetic data help?

Train a force estimator in simulation; freeze it and evaluate it on real sensor recordings with independent ground truth.

WHAT EXISTS

A research foothold, with clear limits.

Prior repositories document an offline vibration-spectrum predictor and a synthetic force-analysis pipeline. Their saved software checks do not establish physical force accuracy, an optical renderer, physical rollouts, or robot control.

Historical result reports are attributed evidence. They have not been rerun by this preparation repository.

CANONICAL SOURCE DATA

Commercial hypothesis

First wedge

A calibrated, repeatable tactile data and evaluation workflow for teams that already operate optical tactile sensors.

Research hypothesis

Longer-term advantage

Measured material/contact assets plus a physical model and learned residual may improve transfer across motions and loads.

Long-term scope

Whole-body touch

Cross-sensor representations, distributed skin, texture vibration, and thermal response remain separate integration challenges.

Platform threat is also an integration question.

MuJoCo, Isaac, Drake, and other engines may be backends, substitutes, or distribution surfaces. The defensible claim must come from customer value and evidence, not “they cannot build touch.”

03 / BUILD PLAN

One sensor.
One repeatable proof.

Build in dependency order. Normal contact comes first; shear and closed-loop control follow the claims you actually need to support.

Personal tracking only

Checking a task records your browser-local progress. It does not verify a result or change a claim’s evidence status.

MINIMUM CREDIBLE DEMO

Measured motion → simulated contact.

Complete the rig, recording, model, held-out comparison, benchmark, and runnable API. Present this as a calibrated simulation prototype. Add synthetic-to-real perception for a stronger utility result.

STRETCH MILESTONE

Feedback before dexterity.

A one-axis motorized stage can maintain force from tactile estimates. A frozen estimator with a conventional controller demonstrates perception transfer and control, not automatically sim-trained policy transfer.
