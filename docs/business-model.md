# Business model and market worksheet

Prepared 26 October 2026. No pricing, market size, revenue, pilots or buyer demand is validated by this document.

## Proposed entry point

**Hypothesis:** sell a workflow for calibrating and validating tactile simulation for one sensor and one real task. A developer package may be the delivery mechanism; the paid value must come from avoided work or improved deployment outcomes.

Start by learning whether users need a simulator, a calibration service, synthetic data, evaluation infrastructure or a combination. A broad platform pitch can hide an unclear purchase decision.

## Commercial options to test

| Option | Unit of sale | Evidence to gather | Cost/risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paid calibration/evaluation pilot | One sensor/task with accepted deliverables | Budget owner agrees to a scoped evaluation | Can become services-heavy |
| Team software subscription | Organization or project | Repeated use across experiments, renewal intent | Needs durable product value and support |
| Enterprise deployment | Annual contract plus support | Integration, procurement and security requirements | Long sales cycle and customization |
| Sensor-vendor licensing | Supported sensor family or deployment | Vendor demand and distribution agreement | Concentration and bargaining risk |
| Metered data generation | Generated samples or compute | Demand for data with demonstrable training utility | Compute costs, commoditization and data rights |

**Pricing hypothesis:** begin with a fixed-fee paid pilot whose price is discussed with the buyer against the actual scope. Test a subsequent per-team annual license if repeated value appears. ## Value and unit economics

For one pilot, estimate:

`customer value = verified engineering hours avoided × customer's loaded hourly cost + avoided hardware/compute cost + supported value of better task outcomes`

Keep the components distinct and attribute estimates. Avoid monetizing an unproven performance improvement. Estimate our engineering, calibration, support, compute, storage and hardware costs. Track gross margin after recurring delivery costs; document founder services that would otherwise be hidden.

## Bottom-up market estimate

Build a named, deduplicated organization list first. Separate the initial serviceable market from a long-term expansion scenario.

`initial annual opportunity = qualifying buying organizations × plausible annual contract value`

`near-term revenue scenario = reachable organizations × evaluation conversion × paid conversion × realized annual contract value`

| Input | Definition | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualifying organizations | Teams with sensor/task/budget conditions matching the initial scope | Count needed |
| Reachable organizations | Teams we can credibly reach through an identified channel | List needed |
| Annual contract value | Buyer-validated price for the agreed scope | Unknown |
| Conversion | Observed transitions with denominator and dates | Unknown |
| Expansion | Additional workflows/sensors with evidence of purchase | Hypothesis |

Do not use the entire robotics market as the tactile-software TAM or multiply every robot by an assumed seat fee. State every assumption and sensitivity. A venture-scale outcome requires a credible expansion path; it is not established by the initial research market alone.

## First customers: a channel experiment, not a forecast

Select one customer segment, identify relevant engineers and budget owners, seek recent workflow examples, offer an evaluation with explicit acceptance criteria, and measure the transition to payment and repeat use. Possible channels to test include sensor-vendor introductions, permissioned research contacts, open-source examples and direct task-specific outreach. No messages or introductions are authorized or sent by this document.

For a "first 100 customers" answer, explain why the first few buy and how their adoption can repeat. Do not claim a scalable channel before observing it.

## Competitive response and defensibility

The alternatives include real-data collection, internal simulator extensions, open-source tools, platform contact features, vendor tooling and simpler controllers. The buyer may rationally choose any of them.

Candidate advantages require evidence: less calibration effort, more reliable transfer at a matched budget, better physical error at matched latency, portable integration or a supported evaluation workflow. A neural network, a five-line API or a photorealistic image alone is not a demonstrated moat. Datasets help only with appropriate rights, provenance, useful coverage and continuing customer value.
